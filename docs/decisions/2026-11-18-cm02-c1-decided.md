# CM-02 C-1 — Architectural Decision

**I approve Option A and select interpretation (b), with one important refinement: operational autonomy is evidence for attributing an Agent Capability, not an automatic OWL classification rule.**

The developer has correctly identified the source of your original concern. CCO's *Skill* class is distinguished by its **human bearer**, not by a uniquely human kind of activity.

I checked the supplied CCO Agent and Event Ontologies. Their definitions support separating the process that realizes a capacity from the bearer of that capacity.

## Decisions on the three questions

| Decision                        | Direction                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Option A**                 | **APPROVED.** Use a common activity-kind description and classify each particular capacity according to its bearer.                                                                                   |
| **2. C-1 test**                 | **APPROVED WITH REFINEMENT. Choose (b), the functional interpretation.** Require positive evidence of autonomous, objective-directed control—not merely installed instructions or automated behavior. |
| **3. Packages and limitations** | **APPROVED.** AI skill packages, agent cards and manifests are information artifacts, not capability bearers. Record CCO's limitations explicitly.                                                    |

There are three corrections I would make before accepting the analysis as the C-1 baseline.

**First, BFO already provides a bearer-neutral superclass: Process (`BFO_0000015`).** What is missing is a verified, appropriately specific mail-sorting process type, not a general bearer-neutral process category. CCO's *Planned Act* is narrower because it requires an Agent and a directive.

**Second, CCO's definition of Planned Act does not universally require that every causative participant personally holds the directive.** It requires at least one Agent to hold it. A stricter requirement for attributing an *artificial system's own Agent Capability* may be a reasonable BCO evidence criterion, but it must not be presented as a CCO axiom.

**Third, the test must not require past execution.** A capability can exist before it is realized. For an artificial system, design, configuration, deployment and operational evidence may establish a capacity without requiring a historical successful performance.

\### 1. Option A — APPROVED\
\
Adopt the conceptual approach that distinguishes:\
\
\- The kind of process a capacity can be realized in.\
\- The particular material entity bearing that capacity.\
\- The applicable bearer-specific CCO classification.\
\- The information artifacts describing or advertising that capacity.\
\
The same general activity kind may be relevant to a person's Skill, an organization's Organization Capability, and an artificial system's Function or Agent Capability.\
\
Do not equate those particular capacities, or assume that sharing an activity kind establishes identical realization conditions.\
\
Do not change existing CCO definitions or introduce new ontology classes or object properties.\
\
\### 2. C-1 — Interpretation (b) APPROVED\
\
Adopt the \*\*functional interpretation\*\* for evaluating whether an artificial material system can bear an Agent Capability.\
\
The assessment must establish that:\
\
1\. A deployed material system is the proposed bearer, rather than merely a software package, model file or descriptive information artifact.\
2\. The system has a capacity to play a causative role in the relevant objective-directed activity, rather than merely being a passive instrument.\
3\. The system can operationally represent and use a directive or plan, select relevant actions toward its objective, and respond appropriately to relevant changes in circumstances.\
4\. The claimed capacity belongs to the deployed system in its actual configuration, rather than being attributed solely from human operators, organizational arrangements, documentation or vendor claims.\
\
These are \*\*BCO's proposed evidence criteria for artificial-system capability attribution\*\*, not a newly asserted OWL equivalent-class definition of Agent Capability.\
\
Apply them case by case.\
\
A thermostat's stored setpoint, a simple fixed automation, an advertised AI skill, or a human operator's instructions do not establish artificial agency merely by existing.\
\
Conversely, do not exclude an artificial system solely because it is nonhuman. Where the evidence establishes the necessary capacity, CCO permits its classification as an Agent Capability, with the consequent Agent classification.\
\
\*\*No prior successful exercise is universally required.\*\* Evidence may support possession of an unexercised capacity. The test concerns what the system is capable of realizing, not whether it has already done so.\
\
CCO's Agent equivalence axiom remains unchanged.\
\
\### 3. Clarify the Planned Act Requirement\
\
Record the distinction between the exact CCO definition and the additional BCO review criterion.\
\
CCO Planned Act requires an Agent to play a causative role and a directive held by at least one involved Agent.\
\
It does not require every causative participant to hold that directive.\
\
When evaluating an artificial system's \*\*own\*\* Agent Capability, evidence of the system's operational control of the relevant directive supports attribution. Do not invent a new formal "holds directive" relation or assume that storing a directive is sufficient.\
\
Avoid circular reasoning in which the system must already have been classified as an Agent before its eligibility for Agent Capability can be assessed.\
\
\### 4. Correct the Statement About Bearer-Neutral Processes\
\
Revise the claimed gap "No bearer-neutral process type."\
\
BFO already supplies the general class \*\*Process\*\* (\`obo:BFO_0000015\`).\
\
The actual gap is the absence, in the presently verified source terminology, of an appropriately specific \*\*mail-sorting process type\*\* that can be used consistently across human and artificial implementations.\
\
Do not introduce such a class merely to complete this analysis.\
\
Do not assume all machine-executed processes are BPO Planned Acts.\
\
\### 5. Information Artifacts — APPROVED\
\
Maintain the distinction between:\
\
\- A material system's actual capability or function.\
\- Software and instruction packages.\
\- Agent Cards and other capability descriptions.\
\- Organizational and human capabilities.\
\
An AI skill package or manifest does not itself bear an Agent Capability simply because it describes executable functionality.\
\
The material deployed system must be evaluated independently.\
\
\### 6. Preserve the CCO Limitations\

\- CCO Skill is bearer-specialized to Person and is not restricted by its class axioms to learned or trained capacities.\

\- Existing realization relations must not be misused to require historical exercise.\

These limitations are part of the architectural analysis, not defects to repair through unauthorized ontology extensions.\

## What it does

- **C-1 is resolved conceptually.** Option A is adopted: an activity kind is described
  once, and each particular capacity is classified by its bearer. Sharing an activity kind
  does not make two capacities equal, and does not give them the same realization
  conditions.
- **Interpretation (b) is adopted, with evidence requirements.** The four criteria in the
  act's section 2 are BCO's evidence criteria for attributing a capability to an
  artificial system, applied case by case. They are not an OWL definition.
  - No prior successful exercise is required.
  - Being nonhuman does not exclude a system.
  - A pass carries CCO's Agent classification with it.
- **Packages, cards and manifests are information artifacts, not bearers.** This is
  confirmed.
- **Three corrections and the limitations go into the analysis as revision 2:**
  - the Planned Act requirement, CCO's definition kept apart from BCO's criterion;
  - the process-type gap, restated as a missing specific mail-sorting process type, with
    BFO Process as the bearer-neutral class;
  - the six limitations.
- **Repository steps authorized:** pushing the branch, opening a pull request, running the
  full suite and CI, and merging the faithful changes once CI passes.
- **The next deliverable** is the next bounded CM-02 conceptual question. It advances the
  Agent Capability model itself, not source discovery.

## What it does not do

- It does not approve CM-02, which still needs its model, competency questions,
  practitioner review and approval under the Stage 02 standard.
- It authorizes no OWL formalization, class, object property, IRI or axiom, and no
  ingestion of external skill catalogues.
- It changes no CCO definition, including Agent's equivalence axiom.
- It does not reopen CM-01, DP-1, DP-2 or the D2 approval.
