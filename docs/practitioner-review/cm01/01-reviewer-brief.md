# Reviewer brief — Business Capability conceptual model (CM-01)

**What we are asking.** Please read a conceptual model of business capabilities and tell us whether it describes organizational capabilities the way they work in practice, and whether it would support real enterprise capability planning. We want your independent professional judgment, including disagreement.

**Who you are in this review.** A domain practitioner: an experienced business architect, enterprise capability planner, or comparable practitioner. You are not expected to know ontology engineering, OWL or BFO. You review in your stated professional capacity, and your name and capacity are recorded with your findings.

**What the model is.** CM-01 is the approved conceptual model behind the Business Capability Ontology (BCO), a formal vocabulary for stating which capabilities an organization has, what they rest on, which business processes exercise them, and what assessments, maps, targets, sourcing and authorization records say about them. CM-01 was approved by the project's architect on 2026-10-26. Your review supplements that approval; it does not repeat it.

**In scope.**

- Whether the model's definitions match organizational abilities you would recognize.
- Whether its boundaries hold up in practice: capability against process, a person's skill, a role or responsibility, an authorization, and the systems and services a capability depends on.
- Whether its treatment of coverage and adequacy of a business function supports actual planning decisions.
- Whether five representative cases behave as you would expect (file 03).
- Whether anything important is missing, and which assumptions you consider wrong.

**Out of scope.** Formal logic, OWL axioms, choice of relations or identifiers, data formats, and the specification's validation rules. You are not asked to approve or ratify anything. The draft specification (v0.5) is supplied for context only; comment on it if you wish, but the review is of CM-01.

**Approved decisions are open to comment.** CM-01 rests on two approved decisions: DP-1 (what a capability rests on, and what it merely depends on) and DP-2 (separating "this kind of capability is genuine" from "this organization has one"). If you think either is wrong for practice, say so. An approved decision is not changed silently: a finding that requires changing CM-01 leads to a new, reviewed version of the model.

**What to do.**

1. Read file 02, a plain-language summary (about 20 minutes). Use CM-01 itself for detail where you need it; section numbers are given throughout.
2. Work through the five scenarios in file 03.
3. Answer the questionnaire in file 04, as briefly or fully as you like. Skip any question you have no view on.
4. Record your findings in the template, file 05, with an example for each where you can, a severity, and a recommendation.

Expected effort: about four to six hours. Return by: `[to be set by the architect]`, through `[to be set by the architect]`.

**What happens next.** Your completed template is filed unchanged as a review record, with your name and capacity, and is published on the project's public site with its other reviews. The architect then decides a disposition for each finding before the specification is ratified. You will receive the dispositions.

**Questions while reviewing** go to `[contact to be set by the architect]`. Please keep the review independent: ask about what the model says, not about what answer is wanted.
