# Five scenarios

Each scenario describes a situation and states what CM-01 does with it, with section references. It then asks what you would expect. The organizations are invented. There is no expected answer. Where your view differs from the model's treatment, please say how and why, in the findings template (file 05).

## S1 — A dormant capability

A regional water utility maintains a major-incident response capability for contamination events. It has had no contamination event in six years.

- Its response team is named.
- Its procedures are current, and it holds annual tabletop exercises.
- Mutual-aid agreements with neighbouring utilities are in place.
- Two of the five team members joined in the last year. The longest-serving member, who led the last real response, retired in March.

**What CM-01 does.**

- A capability that has never been exercised, or not recently, is still a capability (§3 P2; §6, "Capacity / exercise").
- Whether the utility has it is decided by an assessment, which may rest on evidence of capacity alone. That evidence includes members with the skills in the positions concerned, the arrangement in place, and exercises short of real performance.
- A past real response supports the claim but is not required.
- A finding that rests only on past exercise must also show that the people and the arrangement are still in place (DP-2, amendment b5).
- The mutual-aid agreements are sourcing and enabling arrangements, not part of the utility's own capability (§3 P4).

**Questions.**

1. Would you say this utility has the capability today? On what evidence would you rely, and what would make you doubt it?
2. Does the model's line between evidence of capacity and evidence of past exercise match how you would assess a capability like this?
3. Is anything about dormancy, readiness or decay that matters in planning not captured here?

## S2 — A capability resting on one person

A 40-person specialist engineering firm has one treasurer. The treasurer alone produces the firm's 13-week cash forecast and manages its banking facilities. The work follows written procedures and a delegated mandate from the board, and the managing director reviews it monthly. The firm has no deputy for the role.

**What CM-01 does.**

- The firm bears a cash-management capability whose basis is that one member, organized by the firm's arrangement: the mandate, procedures and review.
- One indispensable member can suffice. The capability is organizational because it runs through the organization's arrangements, not because several people share it (§6, "Organizational / one member"; DP-1, amendment a1; DP-2, amendment b1).
- The dependence on one person is recorded as fragility. It does not change what the capability is.
- The treasurer's personal expertise is a Skill, a separate thing from the firm's capability (§4 Skill).

**Questions.**

1. Would you describe this as the firm's capability, the treasurer's, or both? Why?
2. If the treasurer left tomorrow, what would you say the firm has lost, and how would you want a capability map to show it?
3. Is "fragility recorded, not reclassified" a usable treatment in capability planning?

## S3 — Outsourcing

Acme Manufacturing outsources payroll processing to PayCo.

- PayCo's staff run each payroll on PayCo's platform.
- An Acme HR clerk sends PayCo the monthly hours and changes, and receives the payroll report.
- Acme's finance team approves the payment file.
- Acme's capability map lists "Payroll management".

**What CM-01 does.**

- The arrangement is recorded as a sourcing statement: Acme is the consumer, PayCo the provider, and payroll runs are the kind of act PayCo performs (§3 P4).
- Acme does not bear a payroll-processing capability because of the arrangement: PayCo's staff perform those acts.
- Acme may also bear its own capability for the same kind of act. If it does, that capability and the arrangement coexist.
- Acme's other work around payroll, such as sending the inputs and approving the payment file, is not addressed by the arrangement. Whether it amounts to a capability of Acme's is a separate question, decided by its own assessment (§3 P1, P3).
- A separate sourcing assessment records the risk, the substitutability, and whether an internal capability exists.
- Listing "Payroll management" on the map asserts nothing about whether Acme has it (§3 P3).

**Questions.**

1. In your practice, does Acme "have" a payroll capability? If so, what exactly does it have?
2. Does separating the provider's work from the consumer's own capability help or hinder sourcing decisions?
3. What would you need recorded about this arrangement that the model does not provide for?

## S4 — Losing a platform

A building society's mortgage-underwriting team uses a decision platform to score applications. The vendor withdraws the platform with three months' notice, and no replacement will be in place for nine months. The underwriters stay employed, the credit policy and approval authorities are unchanged, and new applications are referred to a partner lender in the meantime.

**What CM-01 does.**

- The underwriters, in their arrangement, are the capability's basis. The platform is an enabling condition.
- Losing the platform, even permanently, does not by itself end the capability. It persists, latent, and is assessed as unavailable until the platform is replaced. It ends only when its basis or its arrangement goes (§6, "The grounding boundary"; DP-1).
- CM-01 records this as the project's own commitment, not as universally true. It notes that NAF, UAF and other enterprise-architecture frameworks count technology inside the capability.
- A capability type for which a technology really is part of the ability is held for a separate open question (§9 Q-15).
- The referral to the partner lender is a sourcing arrangement (§3 P4).

**Questions.**

1. During the nine months, does the building society have a mortgage-underwriting capability? How would you show it on a heat map or capability assessment?
2. Are there capabilities in your experience where losing the technology really does end the capability? Please name an example if you can.
3. Is the model's choice (technology outside the capability) workable for the planning decisions you make? What would it cost you?

## S5 — Functional adequacy

A hospital trust's procurement function ("Manage procurement") requires these component capabilities: sourcing, contracting, purchase ordering, goods receipt and supplier payment. Each one has been assessed as present. Even so:

- requisitions stall between clinical departments and procurement;
- the e-procurement system has had repeated outages this quarter;
- order volumes have doubled since a new hospital wing opened.

**What CM-01 does.** Four separate questions, none inferred from another (§6, "Functional coverage and adequacy"):

1. Possession: does the trust bear every component capability?
2. Integration: do they work together?
3. Availability: are the systems and services in place now?
4. Adequacy: is it able to perform the function to stated criteria?

- Possession is a matter of the individual assessments.
- The other three are findings of a single Functional Adequacy Assessment of the function, each met, partly met or not met against criteria, with evidence (§4 Functional Adequacy Assessment).
- Having every component does not imply having the capability for the function as a whole. A whole-function capability type is used only where one has been defined (§3 P5).

**Questions.**

1. Would these four questions match how you would judge whether the trust can run procurement? Would you add, merge or drop any?
2. Is "every component present, but the function not adequate" a result you would expect to see, and would you trust a model that keeps those answers apart?
3. How would you want this to appear to a planning or investment board?
