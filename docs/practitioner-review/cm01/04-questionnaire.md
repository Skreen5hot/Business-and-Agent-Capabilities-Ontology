# Questionnaire

Answer in your own words, as briefly or fully as you like, and skip any question you have no view on. Where an answer amounts to a finding, please also enter it in the findings template (file 05), with an example.

## A. Conceptual clarity

1. Which concepts in file 02 or CM-01 §4 were unclear, ambiguous, or open to more than one reading? What did you take them to mean?
2. Are the distinctions in CM-01 §6 ("Distinctions the model depends on") ones you could apply consistently? Which would you find hard to apply, and why?
3. Does the model's use of "capability" differ from the usage in your organization or framework? Where would the difference cause confusion?

## B. Business realism

4. Do the definitions of Business Capability and Capability Type describe organizational abilities you would recognize? Please give an example that fits and one, if any, that does not.
5. Are the boundaries between capability and process, a person's skill, a role or responsibility, an authorization, and an enabling system or service defensible in practice? Where do they break down?
6. Do the five scenarios in file 03 behave as you would expect? For any that do not, what would you expect instead?

## C. Practical usefulness

7. Which capability-planning decisions you make would this model support well, and which poorly or not at all? Examples are investment prioritization, sourcing, rationalization, target-state planning, and risk.
8. Would the separation of possession, integration, availability and adequacy (scenario S5) help you make or explain a decision? What would you need from it that it does not give?
9. Could the people who maintain capability maps in your organization record information in this form? What would they find burdensome or unnatural?

## D. Completeness

10. What concepts, distinctions or cases does practitioner capability modeling rely on that CM-01 lacks? Examples are capability levels or decomposition, value streams, information, cost, and ownership. CM-01 §1 lists what is deliberately out of scope, and §9 lists open questions.
11. Are there common situations that the model cannot represent, or represents misleadingly? Examples are shared services, joint ventures, multi-party sourcing, and seasonal or temporary capability.

## E. Problematic assumptions

12. Which assumptions in CM-01 §9 ("Assumptions") or §6 do you consider wrong, risky, or unsupported in practice?
13. Is there anything in the model that would lead a practitioner to a wrong conclusion in a real case? Please describe the case.

## F. The approved decisions

These decisions are approved, and you are free to disagree with them. A finding that requires a change leads to a new, reviewed version of CM-01.

14. **DP-1.** Under DP-1, a capability rests on members in an arrangement, systems and services are things it depends on, and losing a platform does not end the capability. Is that a sound basis for enterprise capability planning? Where would you draw the line?
15. **DP-2.** Under DP-2, admitting a kind of capability is a separate check from attributing one to an organization, and evidence of capacity may suffice without past exercise. Does that separation fit how capabilities are evidenced in practice?
16. Is there anything else in CM-01 that you consider a material concern, whether or not it is covered above?
