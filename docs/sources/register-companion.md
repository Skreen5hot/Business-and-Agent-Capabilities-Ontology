# Sources agree on ability, split on bearer

*Citation register for the Business Capability Ontology, compiled 2026-10-23 by AI research agents (Claude Code) at Aaron Damiano's direction, from the five source notes in [`notes/`](notes/). It is research input to specification v0.4, not a ratified position: entries marked S, K or L below have not been checked against their primary text.*

**No authoritative source defines "business capability" the way the BCO does, but every commitment except the organization-only bearer has citable support, and the disposition reading now has two BFO-family precedents.** This register records 63 sources in five families. The near-universal core is that a business capability is an *ability* of a business: what it can do, not how. That core runs through OMG BACM 1.1 (formal, September 2026), OMG VDML 1.1, OMG UAF 1.3, NAF v4.1, the TOGAF/BIZBOK family and the management literature from Helfat & Peteraf onward. The ontological placement is narrower. **IOF Core (release 202603) and Beverley et al. (2024) put capability under BFO disposition, while CCO v2.0 through the v2.2-era `develop` file keep Agent Capability and Organization Capability under realizable entity.** No standard restricts bearers to organizations. ArchiMate, TM Forum, UAF, ISO 19440, ISO 9000, ITIL, NAF and IOF all admit persons, systems or resources, and BACM's OrgUnit includes individuals. That makes commitment (b) a deliberate narrowing the specification must state as a departure. Commitment (e), ability apart from assignment, has the clearest precedent: the Enterprise Ontology's can/may split, Beverley et al.'s authority-as-role argument, and the VDML and BACM metamodels. Commitment (f), the description layer, is supported structurally, but no source says outright that capability maps or ratings are information about capabilities. Several important primary texts could not be read: TOGAF 10, ArchiMate 4, BIZBOK v15, ISO 19440 clause 7.14 and CMMI v3.0 sit behind logins or paywalls. Their entries here rest on secondary sources and are marked as such.

## How to cite and read this register

Each source has a stable identifier of the form `SRC-<family>-<nn>`. The family codes are ISO (international standards), OMG and OG (consortium standards: Object Management Group, The Open Group), GOV (government and defence), IND (industry reference models and assessment frameworks), FO (formal ontology and conceptual modelling), MS (management science) and BA (business-architecture practice). The identifiers are assigned for this register and carry no meaning outside it. The source notes numbered the BCO commitments differently, so this register uses six named commitments throughout, mapped from each note by meaning:

The management-science note also had a C6 for the specification's three questions (admissibility, possession, adequacy). It is not one of the six commitments. Where it bears on the register it is folded into (f), because adequacy is decided by assessments.

**Verification status** is given for every entry, because the notes differ in how much primary text they read. **V** means the definition was read in the primary text in this research round. **P** means only part of the primary was read: a free preview, a catalogue page, an abstract or a table of contents. **S** means secondary only: the source is quoted or paraphrased by another publication, a vendor or a search-engine extract. **K** means the note author's prior knowledge, not checked; nothing marked K should go into the ontology or the specification until checked. **L** is added when the primary is behind a login, membership, paywall or bot wall and was therefore not read. An entry's status lists its claims, separated by semicolons: each is one of these letters, then, in parentheses, the part of the source it covers, then any note.

**Authority** uses one scale across all families. A1 is an international standard (ISO, IEC, IEEE). A2 is a consortium formal standard (OMG, The Open Group). A3 is government or alliance normative guidance. A4 is a de facto industry standard or body of knowledge. A5 is an open, BFO-aligned community ontology. A6 is a seminal peer-reviewed work. A7 is other peer-reviewed work. A8 is a preprint, practitioner report or practitioner book. A9 is vendor, analyst, grey literature or commentary.

Copyright rule for this register: definitions are paraphrased. Each entry has at most one verbatim quotation, under 15 words and attributed. Nothing here reproduces a copyrighted definition in full, and no source text is vendored.

## Ability is the consensus, but it comes from fewer voices than it seems

Read across families, almost every source calls a capability an *ability* or *capacity*, and almost every business-architecture source adds that it says what the business does, not how. The apparent consensus is thinner than a source count suggests, because the wordings descend from a few originals. **The "particular ability or capacity that a business may possess or exchange" formula traces to Homann's 2006 MSDN paper**. Offerman et al. adopted it almost verbatim, the BIZBOK definition reported in 2017 matches it, and a vendor reports TOGAF 9.2's Business Capability as the same idea ([Offerman et al. 2017](https://liacs.leidenuniv.nl/~plaata1/Offerman2017_SLR.pdf); [Burlton 2017](https://www.brcommunity.com/articles.php?id=b927); [Unicom](https://support.unicomsi.com/manuals/systemarchitect/114100/Architecting_and_designing/TOGAF1495890.html)). TOGAF's business-architecture guides credit Guild practice and Guild graphics, and BACM was developed with the Guild ([Van Haren sample](https://download.boekhuis.nl/9789401809054_fragm-docb.pdf); [BACM 1.1](https://www.omg.org/spec/BACM/1.1/PDF)). **TM Forum's capability framework reuses ArchiMate's definition** ([TM Forum](https://www.tmforum.org/oda/business-capability-framework-2)). NAF v4.1 builds on UAF 1.2, which descends from DoDAF and MODAF ([NATO](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/publications-and-reports/NATO-Architecture-Framework-ArchiMate-v4-1-en.pdf)). In practice there are four independent lines: the Homann–Guild–TOGAF–BACM business-architecture line; the ArchiMate–TM Forum modelling line; the DoDAF–UAF–NAF defence line, which is effect-oriented; and the RBV and dynamic-capabilities line in management science. Citing one source per line is sounder than citing ten from the same line.

The **best single definitional anchor for the BCO is BACM 1.1 §7.3.2.7**: an OMG formal specification from September 2026 whose central concept is the ability a business has to produce an Outcome, without saying how ([BACM 1.1](https://www.omg.org/spec/BACM/1.1/PDF)). It is free to download, current, and keeps capability apart from both behaviour (CapabilityBehavior) and the assignment of authority (PerformerRole). Its Outcome-based identity criterion fits BFO: a disposition's type is fixed by the type of its realizations, and BCO can tie those to BPO act types whose outputs are the Outcomes. In management science, **Helfat & Peteraf (2003)** gives the matching peer-reviewed formulation, an organization's ability to perform a coordinated set of tasks. Its exact wording and page, however, rest on prior knowledge and must be checked before the specification quotes it ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v24y2003i10p997-1010.html)).

None of the business, government or management sources says what *kind* of entity a capability is. VDML and BACM are MOF metamodels whose elements stand for "types or sets" of things; UAF models a Capability as a PropertySet; ISO 19440 models one as a collection of attributes ([VDML 1.1](https://www.omg.org/spec/VDML/1.1/PDF); [UAF 1.3 DMM](https://www.omg.org/spec/UAF/1.3/DMM/PDF); [ISO 19440 preview](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/74491/2045fbd5664f4ea387f29eaa07fd903e/ISO-19440-2020.pdf)). Only the formal-ontology family commits to a category. Within that family, **IOF Core and Beverley et al. choose disposition, CCO chooses the more general realizable entity, the UFO line chooses disposition, and the Enterprise Ontology chooses a relation** ([IOF Core.rdf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/iofoundry/ontology/master/core/Core.rdf); [Beverley et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00183v2); [CCO develop](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CommonCoreOntology/CommonCoreOntologies/develop/src/cco-merged/CommonCoreOntologiesMerged.ttl); [Uschold et al.](https://www.aiai.ed.ac.uk/publications/documents/1998/98-ker-ent-ontology.pdf)).

### How capability relates to process, function, service and value stream

The sources draw these boundaries consistently, even though they define capability differently. **Process**: BIZBOK, Ulrich & Rosen, APQC and BACM all keep capability and process as separate domains linked many-to-many. In BACM the link runs through shared Outcomes and Roles; in APQC, processes are the work and capabilities what is needed to do it ([Burlton](https://www.brcommunity.com/articles.php?id=b927); [Cutter](https://www.cutter.com/article/business-capability-map-rosetta-stone-businessit-alignment-executive-summary-389961); [APQC](https://www.apqc.org/resource-library/resource-listing/business-capabilities-and-how-they-relate-processes)). Eisenhardt & Martin and Winter dissent: for them the capability *is* a routine or process ([Offerman et al.](https://liacs.leidenuniv.nl/~plaata1/Offerman2017_SLR.pdf); [RePEc Winter](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/stratm/v24y2003i10p991-995.html)). **Function**: TOGAF 9.2, as Berrisford reports it, nearly equates a business function with a unit of capability. BIAN's service domains and the FEA BRM partition the business by function and are used as capability taxonomies ([Berrisford](https://grahamberrisford.com/00EAframeworks/03TOGAF/AMTOGpapers/Business%20architecture%20(inc%20capabilities%20and%20value%20streams)%20in%20TOGAF.htm); [ArchiMate-BIAN](https://digital-portfolio.opengroup.org/archimate-bian/latest/01-doc/chap03.html)). In BCO terms these are classification schemes over process kinds, that is, description-layer catalogues. **Service**: DM2 says a service gives access to the activities and resources that produce an effect rather than producing it itself, and ISO 19440 keeps Service as a construct separate from Capability ([DoDAF](https://dodcio.defense.gov/DODAF/dodaf20_capability_mm)). **Value stream**: TOGAF, BIZBOK and BACM treat value streams as how value is delivered and map capabilities to value-stream stages as enablers. In BACM, the capabilities that support a ValueStreamStage are what the business must be able to do to produce value ([Van Haren sample](https://download.boekhuis.nl/9789401809054_fragm-docb.pdf); [BACM 1.1 §7.1.1](https://www.omg.org/spec/BACM/1.1/PDF)). BMM is the outlier: there, processes realize Courses of Action, and capability is only one kind of Means ([BMM 1.3](https://www.omg.org/spec/BMM/1.3/PDF)).

### Maps, assessments, maturity and sourcing

**Capability map or model** is consistently a description. Gartner calls it a representation independent of structure and process. VDML's CapabilityLibrary keeps definitions consistent. NAF's C1 view builds specialization and aggregation taxonomies independent of implementation. Ulrich & Rosen call the map a shared vocabulary ([Gartner](https://gcom.pdo.aws.gartner.com/en/information-technology/glossary/business-capability-modeling); [NATO](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/publications-and-reports/NATO-Architecture-Framework-ArchiMate-v4-1-en.pdf)). **Assessment and maturity** come in two kinds. One rates processes: ISO/IEC 33020 process capability, CMMI capability and maturity levels, COBIT capability levels 0 to 5. The other rates business capabilities: Bizzdesign's maturity and importance scoring, VDML's heatIndex on a CapabilityOffer, and TOGAF's heat maps ([ISO 78526](https://www.iso.org/standard/78526.html); [CMMI Institute](https://cmmiinstitute.com/learning/appraisals/levels); [ISACA](https://www.isaca.org/resources/news-and-trends/industry-news/2020/effective-capability-and-maturity-assessment-using-cobit-2019); [Bizzdesign](https://bizzdesign.com/blog/improving-the-use-of-capabilities-in-business-architecture/)). The UFO line alone puts maturity *inside* the capability, as a quality called the capability level ([Calhau et al.](https://conferences.big.tuwien.ac.at/biweek2024/pdfs/biweek2024_paper_150.pdf)). **Sourcing** enters through "possess or exchange" (Homann, BIZBOK, Offerman), through TOGAF's third parties that possess or use capabilities, and through VDML's split between the provider OrgUnit and the method owner. Khosroshahi et al. list sourcing decisions among the uses of a map ([HICSS](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/handle/10125/50470)). None of these sources says that a capability moves between bearers when it is "exchanged". The BCO reads exchange as a sourcing relation recorded in a description, with the provider bearing its own capability.

**Fault line 1: the bearer, commitment (b).** No source in any family restricts capability bearers to organizations. ArchiMate and TM Forum name person and system; UAF's Exhibits relation admits any CapableElement; ISO 19440 assigns provided Capability to Resources; ISO 9000 to any object; ITIL to people, processes and applications; IOF to any independent continuant; and VDML lets Actors in Positions provide capabilities ([community issue #13](https://community.opengroup.org/archimate-community/workgroups/archimate-feedback/-/issues/13); [UAF 1.3 DMM](https://www.omg.org/spec/UAF/1.3/DMM/PDF); [VDML 1.1 §7.1.7](https://www.omg.org/spec/VDML/1.1/PDF)). Even BACM, whose definition speaks of "a business", has no bearer association, and its OrgUnit includes individuals. Many of these sources do, however, keep a person's ability as a separate concept: UAF Competence, BACM PerformerRole skills, IOF's agential capability (synonym skill), the Enterprise Ontology's SKILL and CCO Skill. That supports the specification's move of sending person-borne abilities to CCO Skill, even though it does not support (b) as a definition of "capability".

**Fault line 2: what is inside the capability, commitment (c).** One camp puts resources inside: Wißotzki, the EA view summarized by Offerman et al., APQC's people–process–information–technology bundle, G211's implementing elements, Grant's team of resources, DM2's ways and means, and CDD's capacity. The other camp keeps them outside: Homann's independence from resources and sourcing, Offerman et al.'s counter-examples, Ulrich & Rosen's rule that automation does not make a capability an IT capability, Amit & Schoemaker's capacity to deploy resources, and ITIL's resource/capability split. **NAF and UAF give the best authoritative analogue of a material basis, the Capability Configuration, but it includes technology**, which (c) excludes ([NATO](https://www.nato.int/content/dam/nato/webready/documents/publications-and-reports/NATO-Architecture-Framework-ArchiMate-v4-1-en.pdf)). No practitioner source states the positive claim that member agents in their arrangement are the basis. It must be argued from Helfat & Peteraf's team origins, Calhau et al.'s emergent capabilities, Beverley et al.'s configured collectives, and BFO's own definition of material basis.

**Fault line 3: ability or routine, commitments (a) and (d).** The disposition reading is opposed by the routine reading (Winter; Eisenhardt & Martin; Dosi, Nelson & Winter), by the relational reading (the Enterprise Ontology), and by the behaviour-element reading in ArchiMate, which UFO analyses reject. The routine camp can be accommodated by treating routines as the arranged pattern in the basis and their enactments as realizations, so that (d) holds.

**Fault line 4: homonyms, commitment (f).** "Process capability" (ISO/IEC 33020, CMMI, COBIT) is a quality or rating of a process, and ISO 9000's Note 1 points to a third, statistical sense in ISO 3534-2. FIBF's "business capability" is a requirement record, and TOGAF's capability-based-planning "Capability", with its increments, is a plan unit. In BCO terms all of these are description-layer entities, not dispositions.

**Fault line 5: specialization or decomposition.** BACM 1.1 forbids specializing capabilities and allows only strict decomposition. UAF has CapabilityGeneralization, NAF C1 allows specialization taxonomies, and VDML allows category hierarchies while admitting the boundary is fuzzy ([BACM 1.1 §7.3.2.7](https://www.omg.org/spec/BACM/1.1/PDF)). Calhau et al. separate decomposition by detailing from decomposition by partitioning, which is a usable account of what map hierarchies mean.

**Fault line 6: ability against assignment, commitment (e).** This is the one commitment no source contradicts. Winter's "decision options for management" and Wißotzki's role/actor elements blur it, but neither denies it. The Enterprise Ontology, Beverley et al., VDML (provider against method owner) and BACM (PerformerRole assignment confers authority) all draw the line explicitly. The management literature is silent on it.

## What this means for specification v0.4

These findings bear directly on the draft. None of them is a decision: CD and CAP items stay proposals until the architect ratifies them.

## Conclusion

The literature does not contain a definition the BCO can adopt; it contains components the BCO has to assemble and defend. The "ability of a business" core is safe to cite from BACM 1.1 and Helfat & Peteraf. The disposition category is defensible from IOF Core and Beverley et al., but only as a commitment beyond CCO's. The ability/assignment split is the best-supported commitment of all. The two commitments that most need argument rather than citation are the organization-only bearer and the member-agent basis, because the field's mainstream, and especially the defence frameworks with their Capability Configurations, puts persons, systems and technology on the capability side. The specification is stronger if it presents both as deliberate narrowings with named departures than as positions the sources already hold.

Two practical risks stand out. First, `has material basis` silently entails disposition, which can undo the CD3 admission gate unless basis assertions wait for G1-T. Second, the sources BCO would cite are moving: ArchiMate 4, BIZBOK v15, BACM 1.1, CCO v2.2 and PCF 8.0 all appeared in 2026. Several of the most-cited primary texts are behind logins the register could not pass. Until GAP-01 to GAP-04 are closed, every TOGAF, ArchiMate, BIZBOK and ISO 19440 clause in this register should be treated as provisional.
