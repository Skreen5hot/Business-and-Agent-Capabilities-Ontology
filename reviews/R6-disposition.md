## What the review could not see

The reviewer worked from the generated Vocabulary page and from BFO 2020 and CCO 2.0 reference PDFs. The page shows each concept's definition, sources and status, but not the specification it wraps or the repository behind it. So the review asks how type references work, what the realization index is, how catalog concepts relate to OWL classes, and how absence is recorded. Specification v0.4 answers each of these, in CD3, CD4, CD8, CD12, CD14 and CD15. Where a row below says "Answered by spec", it cites the clause; it does not claim the reviewer overlooked something they were shown. The review says itself that the page alone is no evidence the OWL semantics are correct (§5), and it offers to review the generated Turtle next (§9). The offer is accepted below.

## Notes on particular rows

- **R6-08, and G1-T question 3.** The removed clause echoed G1-T question 3 almost word for word: "Do realizations of this type run through coordination across organizational positions, rather than one member's skill alone?" G1-T asks it once, of a class; the definition stated it of every instance, which is where it conflicted with CD2. The reviewer's one-analyst consulting firm is a case for G1-T too. It is recorded here for spec v0.5 and RP-10, not settled.
- **R6-27, and the locators.** The research register, `docs/sources/register.yaml` entry SRC-FO-01, records CCO v2.1, v2.2 and a `develop` file of 2026-08-13 as having been read. The vocabulary's locators for SRC-FO-01 repeat "and develop versionIRI 2026-08-13", which is how the reviewer saw those versions on the page. No term is built against them: every upstream IRI the build resolves comes from the vendored 2024-11-06 extract. Narrowing those locators to v2.0 alone is a citation edit the architect may want; it is not made here.
- **R6-05.** ADR-003 D1 (sibling namespaces) and D4 (`bco:designatesClass`) carry this answer. D3 fixes a concept's IRI to its meaning, but it does not bear on punning.
- **R6-07 to R6-12, and ADR-003 D3.** D3 says an editorial change keeps the concept IRI and a change of meaning mints a new one. The revisions are treated as editorial: each catalog concept names the same capability as before, and the definition now states it without excluding cases it was always meant to cover. The architect may judge a revision a change of meaning, in which case D3 requires a new IRI. Nothing was ratified, so no act is broken either way. Each record carries `revises`, pointing at its P5 record digest, and its record digest has changed.
