## What the review could not see

The reviewer worked from the five Turtle files and the BFO and CCO references, and says so in the review's last paragraph: there was no generator, no source YAML, no full specification and no historical record store. So the review could not check that a record digest matches its YAML (the generator and the YAML were missing), could not follow a revision chain (git history was missing), and could not see that spec v0.4 already designs the type reference (CD14, `ex:concernsType`) and places the class hierarchy behind admission (CD3, G1-T). Where a row below says "Answered by spec", it cites the clause; it does not claim the reviewer overlooked something they were shown. Several of R7's points are about what the published graph lets a reader recover without the repository, and the changes here are aimed at exactly that: claims, statuses and prior records can now be read from the five files alone.

## Notes on particular rows

- **R7-01, and claim text.** The reviewer asked for the claim's index, text and status as one structured record. The register has no claim text: a claim is a status with an optional scope (the part of the source the status covers) and note, and that is everything published. The claim IRI is minted from the entry id and the claim's position, not from content, so reordering an entry's claims in `register.yaml` renames them. That is the register's order made visible, and the build re-checks every departure's claim index against the register on each run, so a reorder that moves a cited claim below P fails. `bco:claimStatus` is kept as the property name and moved to the claim, because the property now means what its definition always said: the status of a claim.
- **R7-02, and the commit.** `bco:recordCommit` is the commit that **introduced** the record, not the last commit at which it was current: the introducing commit already exists when a record is revised, so the answer never changes afterwards. It holds while branches are merged with a merge commit, as this repository does; a squash merge would drop a branch's introducing commit from `main`. The search needs the full history (CI checks out with `fetch-depth: 0`), and a shallow clone is a build finding rather than a silently different graph. The R6 records' commit is `137ce61`, the R6 branch's own commit, which `main` reaches through the PR #7 merge.
- **R7-07, and ADR-003 D4.** D4 says a catalog definition is authored "with a BFO/CCO genus". Since this change that is the foundational ancestor, and the immediate genus is a designated `bco:` class, by the architect's decision. The architect may want D4's wording revisited when ADR-003 is next amended; it is not edited here, because a ratified ADR changes only through a new one.
- **R7-08 to R7-12, and ADR-003 D3.** As with R6, the revisions are treated as editorial: each concept names the same capability, and the definition now states it without excluding cases it was always meant to cover. Each record keeps its IRI, `revises` its R6 record digest, and adds a `change_note` citing R7 section 4 and the genus decision; the R6 notes stay. All five pass the quote tripwire. The Treasury Management wording follows the reviewer's suggested refinement in substance, and the change note says so.
- **R7-11, and the choice.** "Understands or shapes" was chosen over stating that both are intended. The definition names the kinds of planned act the capability is realizable in, and an act of either kind realizes it; reading the conjunction as requiring both repeats the problem R6 section 3 removed, where listed practices became conditions that must all hold. The cost, stated in the change note: an organization that only studies its customers' experience now has the capability. The architect may reverse it.
